Module 1 · about 5 minutes

Gear, Beans & Ratios on a Budget

Identify the gear you need, the right grind size and a baseline coffee-to-water ratio.

Minimal gear (under €20 extra)

You probably own most of this already. Even if you buy everything, it stays under €20.

  • A large glass jar (1–2 L) or a pitcher
  • A fine-mesh sieve
  • Paper coffee filters or a clean cotton cloth
  • A basic kitchen scale
  • Optional: a French press can replace the jar and the sieve

Choose and grind your beans

  • Grind: extra-coarse, like coarse sea salt. Fine grounds over-extract and give a muddy, bitter cold brew.
  • Pre-ground coffee: choose one labelled for French press or coarse brewing.
  • Roast: many people like medium to dark roasts for cold brew; light roasts taste brighter and fruitier. Try what you like.

The baseline ratio: 1:4

Cold brew is made as a concentrate that you dilute later (Module 3). Use 1 part coffee to 4 parts water, by weight: for example, 100 g of coarse coffee and 400 g (400 ml) of water. Weigh, don’t scoop: a cup of coffee grounds weighs more or less depending on the grind.

Module 2 · about 5 minutes

Steeping & Filtration

Select the right steeping time and filtering method for a batch.

Lesson video, 1 min 44, with subtitles. The presenter, clips and music are AI-generated.

Combine and steep

  1. Put the coarse grounds in the jar and pour the water over them.
  2. Stir gently until all the grounds are wet.
  3. Cover the jar with a lid or a small plate.
  4. Steep: 12–16 hours at room temperature, or 18–24 hours in the fridge. Never more than 24 hours.

Why the difference? Cold slows extraction, so fridge brewing takes longer.

Filter twice

  1. First pass: pour through the fine-mesh sieve to catch the big grounds.
  2. Second pass: line the sieve with a paper filter or a clean cloth and pour again, slowly.

Tip: let it drain on its own rather than squeezing the cloth, which can push fine particles into your coffee.

Module 3 · about 5 minutes

Diluting, Storing & Troubleshooting

Brew a first batch of ready-to-drink cold brew using the course’s ratio, dilution and storage guidelines.

Dilute

  1. Pour some concentrate into a glass with ice.
  2. Add the same amount of water or milk (1:1).
  3. Taste. Too strong? Go up to 1:2.

Store

  • Concentrate: in a sealed glass jar in the fridge. Best within a week, up to 2 weeks.
  • Diluted cold brew: drink it within 2–3 days.
  • Write the brew date on the jar.

Fix your next batch

Your cold brew is…Try this next time
Too bitterA coarser grind or a shorter steep
Too weakMore coffee or a longer steep
SourA longer steep
Flat, staleA fully sealed jar with little air inside

  • I diluted 1:1 and adjusted to taste
